Judge Dismisses Elon Musk's $150B Lawsuit Against OpenAI After Advisory Jury Finds Claims Time-Barred

Regulation May 19, 2026·NPR

A nine-member advisory jury in California federal court unanimously found that Elon Musk's claims against OpenAI, Sam Altman, and other defendants were time-barred, and Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed, dismissing the case on Monday, May 18, 2026. The jury's finding came in under two hours — an unusually fast deliberation for a case this size.

What Musk was suing for

Musk filed a $150 billion lawsuit alleging that OpenAI and Altman had betrayed the organization's original nonprofit mission. Musk was one of OpenAI's founding donors and board members, leaving the board in 2018. His remaining claims at trial were breach of charitable trust and unjust enrichment, centered on OpenAI's 2019 transition to a "capped-profit" structure and its subsequent commercial partnership with Microsoft. The $150 billion figure represented alleged damages from OpenAI's rise in value since the allegedly improper pivot.

Why the case was dismissed

The advisory jury found that Musk's claims were time-barred — he had waited too long to sue after the events he was challenging — and Judge Gonzalez Rogers adopted that finding in dismissing the case. The statute of limitations ruling meant the jury never evaluated the merits of whether OpenAI actually violated its founding mission. The dismissal does not settle the underlying question of whether OpenAI's commercialization was proper — it simply rules that Musk cannot pursue those claims in court at this point.

What happens next

Musk announced immediately after the ruling that he will appeal to the Ninth Circuit, calling the basis for dismissal a "calendar technicality." Separately, Musk's own AI company xAI continues to compete directly with OpenAI with its Grok model family. Musk's lawsuit had sought to unwind OpenAI's 2025 restructuring; with the case dismissed, that restructuring stands, and the ruling removes a legal overhang that had been cited as a distraction for OpenAI's leadership.
