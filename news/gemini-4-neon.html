Gemini 4 Neon: Google's Mid-Tier Model Arrives for AI Pro and Ultra Subscribers and in the API at $3/$15

Models Nov 18, 2026·Google Blog

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, a third model in the Gemini 4 family. Google places it between Hydrogen, the fast model for everyday tasks that came out a week earlier, and Carbon, the strongest model for the hardest long-running projects. In Google's words, Neon "balances depth and efficiency": it is meant for complex coding, analysis and business workflows where Carbon would be slower and more expensive.

Where you can use it

Gemini app: Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can pick Neon from the model menu in Gemini. Free users do not get it. Google says tasks in Gemini can now run on Neon, which handles multi-step jobs faster than larger models and stretches usage limits further.

Developers: Neon is in the paid tier of the Gemini API, in Google AI Studio and Android Studio. AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can also try it in AI Studio and in Google Antigravity. Businesses get it in Gemini for business, through the API or the model menu.

Security teams: a separate version, Neon for cyber, is rolling out over the coming days to members of Google's Fairwind Program for trusted defenders.

Price and features

In the API, Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens; cached input costs $0.15 per million, a 95% discount. Google's pricing page states that Neon is not available on the free tier. The context window is 1 million tokens, and the model has four thinking levels: low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh.

For comparison, Hydrogen costs $1.50/$7.50, and Carbon costs $2/$10 until November 30 and $4/$20 from December 1. Google says Neon at its default level costs 32% less per task than Carbon.

How it performs, by Google's figures

At the xhigh level Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, third overall behind larger, slower models. Google also reports second place on the Vals Index and first place on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index. In Antigravity, Neon can work as an "orchestrator" that directs several Hydrogen agents in parallel. These are the company's own claims; independent tests will follow.

On the same day, Google rolled out Genie 4, a model that generates interactive 3D worlds you can edit with text prompts, to eligible AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie.

What this means if you are choosing an AI assistant

On AI Pro, try Neon for hard tasks. Long documents, spreadsheets and multi-step research are where Neon should beat the default models. Our Gemini vs ChatGPT comparison helps if you are deciding which subscription to keep.

For developers, it is a middle option on price. Neon costs double Hydrogen. Until November 30 it is even pricier per token than Carbon's introductory rate, but from December 1 it will be cheaper than Carbon. For high-volume simple tasks, Hydrogen remains the cheaper choice; for other options, see our list of AI tools for coding.
