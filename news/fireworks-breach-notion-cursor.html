Breach at AI Vendor Fireworks Hits Notion AI and Cursor, Fake Claude Code Extension Reached Up to 5,000 Users

Tools Nov 13, 2026·Cursor, Notion

A security incident at Fireworks, an AI infrastructure provider that both Notion and Cursor used, exposed some Notion AI chat transcripts and historical coding-agent traces from Cursor. Through credentials in that data, the attacker also planted malicious copies of the Claude Code extension in Cursor's marketplace. Notion published its advisory on November 9, Cursor its full investigation on November 13.

What happened at Cursor

According to Cursor, the attacker obtained an access key that Cursor had issued to Fireworks and that Fireworks then exposed. With it, the attacker copied data from a read-only storage bucket Cursor used to share data with Fireworks. Credentials inside it gave access to some SaaS tools Cursor uses.

On October 29, with a stolen credential, the attacker placed malicious copies of the Anthropic.claude-code extension, versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313, on the cache that serves the Cursor marketplace. They stayed there for about 11 hours, and at most 5,000 users who requested those versions that day could have received them. Anthropic did not publish these builds, the flaw was not in Claude Code itself, and Open VSX, the source of Cursor's marketplace, never had the malware.

The exposed data includes coding-agent traces, mostly from 2025, from sessions not in Privacy Mode (fewer than 0.05% of those users); some traces contained unredacted credentials. API credentials were exposed for fewer than 0.01% of customers. The attacker also reached archived non-production repositories, some support emails and credentials users had sent to support. Cursor says prompts and code from Privacy Mode users, customer repositories, its production systems, the Cursor app and its official updates were not affected, and no release signing keys were exposed.

What happened at Notion

Fireworks first contacted Notion on November 2. Notion's initial review found only synthetic test data, but as a precaution it disconnected from Fireworks on November 3 and deleted the data held there. On November 6 Fireworks reported that customer data may have been accessed, and on November 8 it confirmed a dataset of Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature. Fewer than 1% of workspaces are affected. For most of them, the transcripts carry no author or workspace label and contain no tool results. Notion says its own systems were not breached and no other features were affected.

How the companies responded

Cursor moved all inference off Fireworks, rotated every exposed credential plus all internal ones as a precaution and rebuilt its marketplace store. Notion AI has switched off Insights during the investigation, stopped serving open-weight models through Fireworks and emailed affected customers, who can review the impacted data inside their workspace.

What this means if you use AI tools

Check your Cursor extensions. If you installed or updated Anthropic.claude-code to 2.1.312 or 2.1.313 from Cursor's marketplace, follow the steps in Cursor's October 29 security notice. Cursor warns that uninstalling the extension alone may not be enough.

Rotate what you were told to rotate. If Cursor or Notion notified you, change the keys and passwords they listed. If you pasted passwords or API keys into Notion AI during the incident period, rotate those too. Notion says no action is needed if it has not contacted you; Cursor says that if you were not notified, your data was not in the exposed traces.

Turn on Privacy Mode and keep secrets out of chats. Prompts and code from Cursor users in Privacy Mode were not exposed. Never paste live passwords or API keys into an AI chat.

Look beyond the product when choosing a tool. Both incidents started at the same subcontractor, one that users of either app never dealt with directly. Before adopting an AI tool for work, check its list of subprocessors and what it does with your prompts.
