About Elicit

Elicit is a research intelligence platform built for academics, evidence synthesis teams, and anyone doing serious literature work. It searches a corpus of more than 138 million papers and 545,000 clinical trials, summarizes findings, lets you chat with papers, and pulls specific data points into structured tables.

The tool has changed significantly. What began as a paper-search and extraction assistant is now a broader platform. Research Agents launched in late 2025 to automate multi-step review workflows, and in March 2026 Elicit released a public API so teams can embed its search, extraction, and report generation into their own tools. The underlying language model was upgraded for more accurate, context-aware summaries and better citation handling.

The core paid offering centers on systematic literature review and data extraction: you can screen thousands of papers, define custom extraction columns, and generate structured reports with sources you can verify.

Pricing is tiered. The Free plan costs $0 and includes unlimited search, unlimited summaries, unlimited chat-with-papers, source viewing and Zotero import, with limited Research Agent and Report usage. Pro is $49/month (or $588/year) and covers standard agent, report, and systematic-review usage, screening up to 5,000 papers, 20 table columns, extraction from up to 135 sources, 10 alerts, and API access. Scale is $169/month (or $2,028/year) with 5x usage, figure extraction, live collaboration, up to 200 sources and admin controls. Enterprise is custom-priced with 40,000-paper screening, PRISMA-grade accuracy, SSO/SAML/2FA and unlimited API.

Limitations are worth noting: the tool is tuned for academic and clinical literature rather than general web research, serious extraction and screening volumes require a paid plan, and as with any AI summarizer, extracted claims and citations still need human verification.

Elicit is a strong fit for researchers, PhD students, systematic reviewers, and R&D or clinical teams who need to move quickly through large bodies of evidence without losing the ability to trace every claim back to its source.

  • You need coverage of clinical trials alongside published papers

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Elicit?
Elicit is an AI research assistant designed for systematic literature reviews. It searches academic databases, screens papers for relevance, and extracts structured data into tables — automating the most time-consuming parts of academic research. It's used by researchers, scientists, and evidence synthesis teams.
What is the difference between Elicit and Consensus?
Elicit is built for systematic reviews — it extracts structured data from papers into tables for meta-analysis. Consensus is built for fast Q&A — it synthesizes findings from papers into plain-language answers with a consensus meter. Elicit is for deep research workflows; Consensus is for quick evidence-based answers.
Is Elicit free?
Elicit has a free plan with 5,000 credits per month (roughly 500 paper summaries). Elicit Plus is $12/month for 15,000 credits, and higher tiers are available for teams and institutions.
Can Elicit replace a research assistant?
Elicit can handle the mechanical parts of systematic reviews: paper screening, data extraction, and summarization. It significantly reduces research assistant hours for literature reviews. However, expert judgment for quality assessment and interpretation of findings still requires human researchers.
